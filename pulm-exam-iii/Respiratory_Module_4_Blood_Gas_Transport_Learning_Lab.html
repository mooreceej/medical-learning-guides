1. Start here

Reasoning sequence: identify what is being measured → separate pressure from content → decide whether the issue is Hb amount, Hb saturation/affinity, or dissolved gas → predict tissue unloading.

Fast rule: PaO₂ = dissolved O₂ pressure while CaO₂ ≈ Hb-bound O₂ under ordinary conditions because Hb carries the overwhelming majority of O₂.

Why the curve is sigmoidal

Positive cooperativity: binding the first O₂ increases Hb’s affinity for subsequent O₂ molecules. This supports near-full loading in the lungs and efficient unloading across the steeper tissue-range portion of the curve.

Professor emphasis: the arterial/alveolar PO₂ can fall from about 100 toward 60 mmHg with relatively little loss of saturation because this is the flatter upper portion of the curve.

3. Concept guide

O₂ carriage

Dissolved: 0.003 mL O₂/dL/mmHg. At PaO₂ 100 → ~0.3 mL/dL.

Hb-bound: 1.34 mL O₂ per gram Hb. At Hb 15 g/dL and 100% saturation → 20.1 mL/dL.

Total possible in the lecture example: ~20.4 mL/dL = 20.1 bound + 0.3 dissolved.

Pressure vs content

PaO₂ asks: “How much free/dissolved O₂ tension is present?”

SaO₂ asks: “What percentage of Hb O₂ sites are occupied by O₂?”

CaO₂ asks: “How many mL of O₂ are actually carried per dL?”

Right shift — unload

↓ affinity • ↑ P50 • ↑ O₂ unloading

Lecture scenarios: ↑CO₂, ↓pH, ↑temperature, ↑2,3-DPG; active tissue; high altitude adaptation via ↑2,3-DPG.

Think: “the tissue is asking for oxygen.”

Left shift — hold

↑ affinity • ↓ P50 • ↓ O₂ unloading

Opposite local conditions: ↓CO₂, ↑pH, ↓temperature, ↓2,3-DPG. CO poisoning also left-shifts the remaining O₂-binding sites.

Think: “Hb is less willing to let oxygen go.”

Bohr vs Haldane

Question to askLecture logic
Bohr effectWhat does CO₂/H⁺ do to O₂ binding?More CO₂ / lower pH → lower O₂ affinity → right shift → O₂ unloading.
Haldane effectWhat does O₂ loading/unloading do to CO₂ carriage?Deoxygenated Hb can bind/carry CO₂ more readily; oxygenation in the lungs promotes CO₂ release.

Carbon monoxide: the double hit

Hit 1 — capacity

CO competes for heme sites with roughly 200–250× the affinity of O₂. Occupied sites cannot carry O₂.

Hit 2 — unloading

CO increases the O₂ affinity of remaining unoccupied Hb sites → left shift → impaired tissue unloading.

Important distinction: PaO₂ reflects dissolved O₂. CO poisoning can therefore produce major tissue hypoxia without PaO₂ falling in proportion to the loss of functional Hb carriage.

CO₂ transport

The transcript describes three forms: bicarbonate (majority), dissolved CO₂, and carbamino compounds such as carbaminohemoglobin.

Dissolved CO₂ ≈ 0.07 mL CO₂/dL/mmHg × PCO₂
At PCO₂ 40 mmHg → 2.8 mL CO₂/dL

Master distinctions

PairFast discriminator
PaO₂ vs CaO₂Pressure = dissolved gas; content = mostly Hb-bound O₂.
Anemia vs CO poisoningAnemia: fewer Hb molecules. CO: Hb sites occupied + remaining sites left-shifted.
Right vs left shiftRight releases O₂ (↑P50); left holds O₂ (↓P50).
Bohr vs HaldaneBohr: CO₂/H⁺ changes O₂ affinity. Haldane: O₂ loading state changes CO₂ carriage.
SaO₂ vs Hb concentrationSaturation is a percentage; Hb concentration determines how many binding sites exist.

Question: Why does a fall in PO₂ from roughly 100 toward 60 mmHg cause relatively little change in Hb saturation, while a further drop causes a much larger change?

Interpretation: the upper curve is a plateau, so saturation changes slowly across that PO₂ range. The lower-middle portion is steeper, allowing efficient unloading in tissues.

Exam trap: equating a change in PaO₂ with an equally large change in O₂ content or saturation.

High-yield traps

  • Normal SaO₂ does not exclude anemia. Saturation is a percentage; anemia reduces the number of Hb molecules.
  • PaO₂ is not total O₂ content. It reflects dissolved O₂.
  • Right shift = lower affinity. At the same PO₂, saturation is lower because Hb lets O₂ go.
  • CO poisoning is not just “blocked binding.” It also left-shifts remaining sites and impairs unloading.
  • Bohr ≠ Haldane. Bohr: CO₂/H⁺ changes O₂ affinity. Haldane: O₂ loading state changes CO₂ carriage.
  • 100% O₂ raises dissolved O₂ but does not restore missing Hb in anemia.

CO poisoning mini-chain

CO binds Hb → available O₂ sites ↓ → O₂ carrying capacity ↓
+ remaining sites hold O₂ more tightly → left shift → tissue unloading ↓ → hypoxia
