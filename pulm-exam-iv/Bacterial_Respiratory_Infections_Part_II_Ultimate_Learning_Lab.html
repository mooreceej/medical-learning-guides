1. Start here

The repeatable pneumonia question algorithm

  1. Is this pneumonia? Look for infection of alveoli/lung parenchyma: compatible symptoms + exam/imaging.
  2. Where was it acquired? Community, ≥48 h after admission, or ≥48 h after intubation.
  3. What exposure/risk pattern changes the organism list? Influenza, close quarters, water, aspiration/alcohol, CF, ventilation, immune compromise.
  4. Typical vs atypical? Focal purulent/lobar pattern versus prolonged dry cough/interstitial pattern or extrapulmonary clues.
  5. What test actually answers the question? Culture/susceptibility, Legionella urine antigen, MRSA nares as a stewardship tool, etc.
  6. Choose therapy by organism biology + setting. No cell wall → no β-lactam; resistant nosocomial gram-negative → antipseudomonal strategy; MRSA pneumonia → vanc/linezolid/ceftaroline, not daptomycin.

Core map

Must know
Pathogen enters alveoli → cytokines/neutrophils rise → exudate + debris + fibrin + fluid fill alveoli → gas exchange worsens → hypoxemia / increased work of breathing.

Classification cutoffs: HAP = pneumonia developing ≥48 h after hospital admission. VAP = pneumonia developing ≥48 h after intubation.

Complications: respiratory failure, shock, lung abscess, parapneumonic effusion, empyema.

8. Exam sheet

Must know core framework
Pneumonia = alveoli/lung parenchyma infection. Pathogen → cytokines/neutrophils → exudate/debris/fibrin/fluid → impaired gas exchange. Classify by setting first: CAP vs HAP (≥48 h after admission) vs VAP (≥48 h after intubation).

CAP organisms

  • S. pneumoniae: most common CAP cause; vaccine available.
  • Mycoplasma: young/close-contact/walking pneumonia; no cell wall → no β-lactam.
  • H. influenzae: pneumonia + epiglottitis/otitis/bronchitis; Hib vaccine.
  • C. pneumoniae: atypical CAP; azithro/doxy/clarithro/resp FQ.
  • Legionella: water, GI symptoms, hyponatremia; urine Ag limited to serogroup 1.
  • S. aureus: especially after influenza; necrotizing/cavitary possible.

HAP/VAP + severe gram-negative clues

  • Pseudomonas: VAP/CF/immune compromise/foreign bodies; resistance; toxin A inhibits EF-2.
  • Klebsiella: alcohol use, aspiration, hospitalization; capsule; necrotizing/cavitary disease.
  • MRSA pneumonia: vancomycin, linezolid, ceftaroline; not daptomycin because surfactant inactivates it.

Trap list
Moxifloxacin ≠ Pseudomonas coverage in the lecture table. Ertapenem ≠ antipseudomonal carbapenem. Daptomycin ≠ MRSA pneumonia. Positive MRSA nares ≠ proof of MRSA pneumonia. Legionella urine antigen ≠ every Legionella. Mycoplasma ≠ β-lactam target.
