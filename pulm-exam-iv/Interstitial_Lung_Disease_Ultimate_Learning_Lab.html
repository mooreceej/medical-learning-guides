1. Start here

The lecturers repeatedly frame ILD as a multidisciplinary pattern-recognition problem. Start by deciding where the injury lives, then identify what fills or scars that compartment, then add the clinical context.

Must know

The 5-step ILD question algorithm

1 Confirm restriction→2 Read distribution→3 Name the pattern→4 Find the cause/context→5 Choose diagnosis + treatment logic
  1. Restriction: ↓TLC/↓FVC/↓VC, normal or ↑ FEV₁/FVC, often ↓DLCO.
  2. Distribution: subpleural/basilar? bronchiolocentric? diffuse? intra-alveolar? upper-lobe?
  3. Pattern: patchwork scar, uniform septal disease, granulomas, fibroblastic plugs, smoker macrophages, dust pigment?
  4. Context: drugs, birds/mold, CTD clues, smoking, occupation.
  5. Meaning: inflammatory disease may respond to immunosuppression; established fibrosis does not reverse.
Normal → abnormal bridge

Why the interstitium matters

Normal alveolar septa are extremely thin and contain capillaries for gas exchange. In ILD, cells and/or collagen thicken or replace this interface. That produces impaired diffusion and, as scar accumulates, a smaller/stiffer lung.

Course language: the primer groups patterns broadly into interstitial patterns (e.g., UIP, NSIP, bronchiolocentric patterns, DAD) and alveolar-filling patterns (e.g., organizing pneumonia, RB-ILD/AMP).

History buckets

Drug: methotrexate, amiodarone, nitrofurantoin, sulfasalazine, bleomycin, cyclophosphamide, immune checkpoint inhibitors, radiation, oxygen toxicity. Environment: birds, hot tubs, mold, coolers. Occupation: mining, sandblasting, stone cutting, shipbuilding, HVAC/plumbing/construction.

CTD clues

Raynaud phenomenon, inflammatory arthritis/arthralgia, rash/skin lesions, mucosal ulceration. These can turn a generic NSIP pattern into a CTD-associated ILD diagnosis.

Exam + imaging

Velcro-like crackles, squeaks/squawks, clubbing, and late pulmonary-hypertension signs may occur—but exam can be normal. HRCT is the main imaging tool; expiratory images help detect air trapping and prone images help dismiss dependent atelectasis.

What distribution and structural changes make this pattern favor UIP?

Look for peripheral/subpleural and basilar fibrosis with honeycombing/traction bronchiectasis rather than diffuse ground glass.

Trap Calling any fibrosis 'IPF'—UIP is a pattern, and secondary causes must still be excluded.

Figure 2

What single histologic organization pattern is the high-value clue here?

Patchwork fibrosis: dense scar sits next to relatively preserved lung, creating temporal/spatial heterogeneity typical of UIP.

Trap NSIP is diffuse/uniform; UIP deliberately preserves islands of normal lung beside scar.

Source after reveal: Restrictive lung disease slides, slide 24

Figure 3

Which CT features would pull you toward NSIP rather than classic UIP?

Ground-glass opacity with reticulation/traction bronchiectasis and less honeycombing; subpleural sparing can occur.

Trap Ground glass is supportive but not diagnostic by itself—pair it with uniform pathology and CTD/drug context.

Source after reveal: Restrictive lung disease slides, slide 28

Figure 4

Why is an expiratory image especially useful for this pattern?

It can reveal lobular air trapping as mosaic hyperlucency, a major clue to chronic hypersensitivity pneumonitis.

Trap Diffuse AMP/DIP ground glass is more homogeneous; expiratory HP mosaicism follows secondary pulmonary lobules.

Source after reveal: Restrictive lung disease slides, slide 35

Figure 5

What pathology clue separates this from sarcoidosis?

Bronchiolocentric lymphocytic interstitial inflammation with poorly formed granulomas/giant cells favors HP.

Trap Sarcoid granulomas are typically well-formed and BAL favors CD4; HP granulomas are poorly formed and lecture BAL favors CD8.

Source after reveal: Restrictive lung disease slides, slide 39

Figure 6

What are the pale/pink plugs doing, and why does architecture matter?

They are loose organizing fibroblastic plugs occupying bronchioles, alveolar ducts and alveoli. The surrounding lung architecture is preserved, favoring organizing pneumonia.

Trap Dense scar with architectural destruction would argue for UIP rather than organizing pneumonia.

Source after reveal: Restrictive lung disease slides, slide 45

Figure 7

Where are the pigmented macrophages concentrated?

They are centered in respiratory bronchioles—respiratory bronchiolitis / RB-ILD in the right clinical setting.

Trap If smoker macrophages diffusely fill alveolar spaces, think AMP/DIP instead.

Source after reveal: Restrictive lung disease slides, slide 55

Figure 8

What exposure marker is shown and what is it made of?

An asbestos body: an asbestos fiber coated by iron-rich proteinaceous material produced by macrophages; golden-brown and beaded/fusiform with a translucent center.

Trap Pleural plaques do not contain asbestos bodies.

8. Exam sheet

Built to stand alone for final review. Coverage is organized around the decisions you need to make under exam pressure.

Part I — Core framework

Must knowILD = inflammation and/or fibrosis of interstitium and/or alveoli → restrictive physiology + impaired gas exchange. End-stage disease converges on a fibrotic, shrunken/honeycomb lung.
DecisionExam-ready rule
Confirm restriction↓TLC, ↓FVC, ↓VC; FEV₁ also falls but FEV₁/FVC is normal or ↑; DLCO often ↓ in parenchymal ILD.
Best diagnostic toolDetailed history: current/past drugs, vaping/tobacco, birds/mold/hot tubs, job history, CTD symptoms.
ImagingHRCT. Expiratory images expose air trapping/mosaicism (HP). Prone images help distinguish dependent atelectasis from real ILD.
BiopsyNot universal. Often unnecessary with definite UIP on HRCT, clear CTD-ILD, no therapeutic consequence, or very mild watchful disease. Standard transbronchial biopsy has limited value; OP/sarcoid and selected diseases are exceptions.
Multidisciplinary logicHistory + clinical findings + PFT + radiology ± pathology → clinicopathologic diagnosis. Pattern alone is not diagnosis.

Part II — Testable distinctions and transcript nuance

UIP vs NSIP

UIP: patchwork, heterogeneous, architectural distortion, honeycomb, subpleural/basilar. NSIP: diffuse/uniform, same-stage, GGO/reticulation, less honeycomb, possible subpleural sparing, CTD common.

HP vs Sarcoid

HP: organic exposure, bronchiolocentric, poorly formed granulomas, expiratory mosaic air trapping, lecture BAL CD8>CD4. Sarcoid: well-formed noncaseating granulomas, hilar LAD, multisystem, BAL CD4/CD8 >4:1.

RB vs AMP/DIP

RB: smoker macrophages centered in respiratory bronchioles. AMP/DIP: the same pigmented macrophages diffusely fill alveoli; CT becomes more homogeneous ground glass.

OP vs bacterial pneumonia

OP: patchy/migratory opacities, repeated antibiotics fail, loose same-age fibroblastic plugs, preserved architecture, steroids often work.

Stem clueProcessWhat makes it testable

Question-stem decoder

If the stem gives: elderly man + progressive dyspnea + basilar subpleural honeycomb
First identify: UIP pattern
Then check: secondary causes before calling IPF
Avoid: equating UIP automatically with IPF.
If the stem gives: CTD clues + diffuse GGO/reticulation
First identify: NSIP pattern
Then check: uniform pathology / subpleural sparing
Avoid: choosing UIP just because fibrosis exists.
If the stem gives: bird/mold exposure + expiratory mosaicism
First identify: HP
Then check: bronchiolocentric lymphocytes, poorly formed granulomas, CD8-predominant BAL per lecture
Avoid: calling it type I allergy.
If the stem gives: recurrent 'pneumonia' in changing locations despite antibiotics
First identify: organizing pneumonia
Then check: loose fibroblastic plugs, preserved architecture
Avoid: continuing to treat as bacterial infection.
If the stem gives: hilar LAD + noncaseating granulomas + extrapulmonary findings
First identify: sarcoid
Then check: infection exclusion and high CD4/CD8 ratio
Avoid: assuming every granuloma is sarcoid.
If the stem gives: smoker + pigmented macrophages
First identify: distribution
Bronchiole-centered: RB/RB-ILD
Diffuse alveolar: AMP/DIP.

Professor-emphasis pearls

NuanceUIP vs IPF: the lecture repeats this distinction multiple times. UIP is an injury pattern; IPF is the idiopathic clinicopathologic syndrome.
NuanceDIP terminology: the pathologist calls DIP an older/misleading term and prefers alveolar macrophage pneumonia because the key lesion is smoker macrophages filling alveoli.
TrapHP hypersensitivity type: do not reflex to type I/eosinophils. The slides teach type III and/or type IV.
TrapAsbestos: asbestos bodies support exposure, but pleural plaques themselves contain dense collagen—not asbestos bodies.

Last 90-second pass

Patchwork = UIP. Uniform = NSIP. Bronchiolocentric + poorly formed granuloma = HP. Loose plugs + migrating infiltrates = OP. Well-formed granuloma + hilar nodes = sarcoid. Smoker macrophages in bronchioles = RB; throughout alveoli = AMP/DIP. Black coal pigment progresses from anthracosis → simple CWP → PMF. Silica = upper lobes + TB. Asbestos = plaques/fibrosis + iron-coated bodies.
