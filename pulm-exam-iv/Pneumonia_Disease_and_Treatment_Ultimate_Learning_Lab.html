1. Start here

Use one repeatable decision chain for nearly every question: Is this infection? → where was it acquired? → classical, atypical, aspiration, viral, or VAP pattern? → how sick is the patient? → which diagnostic test changes the answer? → what therapy fits the organism + setting + complication?

Core map

Pneumonia question algorithm

  1. Confirm an infectious lung-parenchyma syndrome; separate pneumonia from noninfectious pneumonitis.
  2. Classify acquisition: community vs hospital/healthcare vs ventilator.
  3. Classify phenotype: classical, atypical, aspiration, viral.
  4. Assess severity: RR, oxygenation, blood pressure/hemodynamics, confusion, age, comorbidities, complications, support.
  5. Use CXR/infiltrate plus targeted microbiology; escalate to bronchoscopy/BAL if severe and unrevealing.
  6. Choose therapy that covers the likely pathogen profile and recognize major toxicity traps.
  7. Reassess: failure to improve can signal empyema, bacteremia/metastatic infection, wrong organism, or a nonbacterial differential.

Pneumonia Disease & Treatment — Exam Sheet

Part I — Core framework & must-know content

Core framework
Infection? → Acquisition site? → Pattern? → Severity/admission? → Diagnostics? → Pathogen coverage? → Complication/source control?

Definition & susceptibility

  • Pneumonia: infection of lung parenchyma ± airways with local/systemic signs.
  • Pneumonitis: inflammatory lung injury without evidence of infection; infection can cause pneumonitis, but not all pneumonitis is infectious.
  • Higher vulnerability: elderly, underlying lung disease, major comorbidities, immunosuppression, immunoglobulin deficiency, T-cell dysfunction, cirrhosis.

Admission logic

  • Extremes of age, RR >30, hemodynamic instability, abnormal gas exchange, pleural effusion/sepsis, chronic lung disease/comorbidities, poor support.
  • CURB-65 lecture rule: confusion, BUN ≥20 mg/dL, RR >30, SBP <90 or DBP <60, age >65; >2 -> admit.

Diagnostics

  • CXR: lecture note says an infiltrate is needed to diagnose pneumonia; may be uni- or multilobar and may show pleural effusion.
  • Exam: diminished breath sounds, rales/rhonchi, egophony/whispered pectoriloquy, tachypnea.
  • Valid sputum: >25 WBC/high-power field and <10 epithelial cells.
  • Blood cultures: typically two, not drawn at exactly the same time.
  • Legionella urine antigen: detects L. pneumophila; a negative test does not exclude other Legionella species.
  • Severe/refractory + unrevealing basic tests -> bronchoscopy/BAL/brushings.
  • Serology is slower; classic IgG approach requires a fourfold rise; cold agglutinins for Mycoplasma are not very specific.

Part II — Distinctions, complications & stem decoding

Complications

  • Parapneumonic effusion / empyema: pleural complication. Empyema = pus/infected pleural space. Lecture criteria: pH <7.2, glucose <60 mg/dL, LDH >50% serum, bacteria cultured. Drainage is paramount; antibiotics are prolonged.
  • Respiratory failure may require ventilation, which then increases VAP risk.
  • Post-influenza bacterial pneumonia: “better then worse” after influenza. Mechanism: epithelial injury + impaired macrophage/neutrophil function + type I interferon effects on protective cytokines (e.g., IL-17).
  • Viral complications: bronchitis/bronchiolitis, necrotizing bronchiolitis, viral pneumonia, alveolar hemorrhage, effusion, bronchiectasis, fibrosis, respiratory failure/ARDS, encephalitis, myocarditis, septic shock, Swyer-James, death.

Mortality / severity clues

  • Multilobar disease, elderly age, hypotension, RR >30, underlying lung disease, respiratory failure, certain pathogens.
  • For pneumococcal pneumonia, positive blood culture is associated with increased mortality.

Trap
Professor Practice Q4 nuance: the answer key selects moxifloxacin but explicitly notes azithromycin could also be responsible. The lecture slide warns that both quinolones and macrolides can prolong QT.
Transcript nuance
The transcript calls a parapneumonic effusion “transudative” in one sentence. That appears to be a verbal slip; infection-associated parapneumonic effusions are exudative. For the course, use the slide’s complication framework and empyema criteria rather than memorizing that isolated word.
